## How rule 85's burden of proof was tested, per half

**Half 1.** Stage 2 rests the widening on one claim: the timestamp must come from the same open file object that produced the digest and the byte count. I tested the mechanism rather than the sentence. `_fetch_to_temp` writes a per-process temp and installs it with `tmp.replace(target)` (`bin/sc:2768`), so an installation is an inode swap: a descriptor already open on the target keeps the old inode, and a `path.stat()` issued afterwards describes the new one. The race is therefore real *as a mechanism* — the smaller design really can pair a digest from one inode with an mtime from another, and really can pair an `absent` status with a live age (the display-site `os.stat()` would also need a `try/except OSError` and a decision about the disagreement, which is code stage 2's "about 7 edited lines" does not count).

But I do not think the race is what earns the design, and I would not have approved on it alone. Its window is a weekly timer with a one-hour randomised delay against a hand-typed `sc status`, and its consequence is one status line pairing an old status with a new age. What actually carries half 1 is threefold and none of it is probabilistic: FR-1 and AC-S1 make the single reader binding, so stage 2 could not have chosen the smaller design without a stage-1 rollback; `docs/dev-map.md`'s "Never form a second opinion" and its "Don't add a second notion of 'the rule-set is there'" are standing project rules that the display-site `stat()` violates directly; and T-20 is filed, so the future edit the widening prevents is nameable rather than speculative — the doctor row lands as one `_age_text(mtime)` call inside `_doctor_rulesets()`'s existing loop instead of a second stat site. That is the shape rule 85's counter-rule asks for ("if you cannot name the future edit it prevents, it is not justified"), and here it is named.

I counted the size honestly before agreeing. Of `bin/sc`'s ≈ +67, about 26 lines are docstring or comment and 7 are translation-table data that FR-10 mandates; the executable remainder is roughly 20 lines for half 1 (one `fstat`, five widened return sites, three destructuring sites, a ten-line renderer, a four-line section) and roughly 14 for half 2. Against T-18's +15/−7 for one exception envelope, half 2 is in the same league and half 1 is about three times it — for a feature that has to touch a five-return-site function, add a renderer and add a screen section. I could not find a way to cut it that does not either violate FR-1 or delete a stated requirement. The one genuine saving I found is A-6's starred unpacking, which is worth zero lines now and three lines at T-20; I left it as a developer choice rather than a condition, because mandating it would be authoring the design rather than judging it.

Stage 2's third rejected alternative in half 1 gives a reason I could not reproduce: it says a `"{n} {unit} ago"` template "renders a stray space in Chinese". A zh value of `"{n}{unit}前"` has no stray space. The ruling survives on stage 2's own arithmetic — four keys with no composition step beats five keys with one — so I recorded F-7 to stop the unsound reason being inherited by the next task that reaches for a unit ladder, and left the ruling alone.

**Half 2.** Here rule 85 does not apply the way stage 2 framed it. The tie-break is between designs that satisfy **the same stated requirement**; the smaller design (fix only the regeneration claim) satisfies strictly less. I checked whether FR-7's third term was stage-1 invention and it is not: the goal sentence in `01` reads "fails to regenerate the config, **or fails to restart the service**", so the restart clause is owner-stated. Q-6 is right that leaving it out ships the T-01 shape — a run that detects its own failure and reports success anyway.

I then priced the difference against HEAD's tail rather than against the design's prose. Over the smaller design, half 2 costs: one hoisted `regen_ok`, one `if regen_ok:` around the restored line, `restarted` moving from `False` to `None`, `restarted = restart_service()` in place of a bare call, one `ok = …` line, one extra outcome branch, one `sys.exit(1)`, and one translation key. That is about seven executable lines and one data row for BC-10, AC-B6 and the failure Q-6 calls the loudest lie the run can tell. Comfortably earned.

I also verified the alternative stage 2 rejected in the other direction — a `failures` list or an exception envelope — is genuinely larger and genuinely forbidden. `failed`, `regen_ok` and `restarted` already exist in that scope, so the one-line conjunction is a read over data the run holds; a list would be a second record of the run's success, which is the disagreement FR-6 exists to prevent, and the envelope form is what Q-2 declined for R-12.

## The RS-4 ruling in full

Stage 2 routed this to me explicitly and it deserves the space.

The NFR reads: "`sc status` gains at most one additional filesystem metadata query per rule-set — four in total, on local files already being read — and no network call, no service call and no subprocess." At HEAD, `cmd_status()` reads no `.srs` file at all; it prints five headings, runs `systemctl status` and `ip -br addr show`, and then, gated on `is_running()`, reads `nodes.json`, asks the Clash API and queries the egress endpoint. The design's new section calls `ruleset_states()`, which chunk-reads and sha256s all four files. Taken literally, "on local files already being read" is false and "no subprocess" is false of the command as a whole (it already spawns two).

Both clauses become true under exactly one reading: the sentence bounds the increment attributable to the **timestamp**, not the cost of the section. Under that reading the timestamp adds one `fstat` on a handle the reader already holds, adds no read of its own, and adds no network call, service call or subprocess — all four clauses hold, and the sentence is internally consistent. Under the literal reading two of its four clauses are false of HEAD before this task changes anything, which is the tell that the subject is wrong rather than the design.

So the defect is stage 1's wording. I did not roll back for it, for three reasons. First, no cheaper shape exists that keeps FR-1's property: to print a rule-set's *status* you must read its bytes — `srs_reject_reason(head, size)` needs the full byte count, and the whole point of `ruleset_state()`'s contract is that `size` is the read's own count and never `st_size` (the second opinion T-05 removed). Reading only a prefix would be a different, weaker status judgment. Second, `ruleset_report()` — the obvious "reuse an existing seam" move — costs exactly the same, because it *is* `_status_view(ruleset_states())`. Third, the absolute cost is small: `SRS_MIN_BYTES` is 16 and the four files are `geoip-cn`, `geosite-cn`, `geosite-google`, `geosite-private`, which on this project's mirror are tens to a few hundred kilobytes — well under the 8-second egress query `sc status` already performs.

That last sentence is exactly the kind of claim this project has been burned by (T-09's timer history, T-18's batch-goal cause), so I refused to let it ship as a premise. C-2 turns it into a measurement: the fixture byte totals and five-run medians at HEAD and candidate, with a stated threshold above which it is a defect rather than a footnote. A gate that rules on a cost claim without a number is doing what T-09 did.

I record the counter-consideration for whoever revisits this: after T-20, this project will render per-rule-set status on two screens and derive the age on both. That duplication is requirement-mandated (FR-3 for `sc status`, T-20 for `sc doctor`) and it is *not* a second opinion — both consume `ruleset_states()` and `_status_text()` — but it is the kind of thing a third surface would make untenable. FR-2's "one renderer, no command-specific argument" is what keeps it from becoming one.

## First-hand checks — what held, and the one that did not

Every line reference and structural claim in `02` was read rather than trusted, and all of them hold. `_status_view()`'s 3-element return really does stop the widening — the destructuring sites downstream are `_runtime_overlay():1786`, `usable_tags():894` and `_warn_degraded():947`, all three taking `(tag, fname, status)`. `ruleset_status():823` really does index `[0]` and survives. `changed_usable_tags()` really does pair through dicts keyed by tag (`:876`, `:878`) and not by list index, so the widening cannot silently mis-pair. `restart_service()` really has exactly two callers, and the six commands stage 2 lists (`sc reload`, `use`, `add`, `rm`, `ipv6`, `telemetry`) really do reach the service only through `reload_or_restart()` — I resolved each of `:2158`, `:2180`, `:2196`, `:2627`, `:2690`, `:2927` individually rather than counting them. The unit really is `Type=oneshot` with one un-prefixed `ExecStart`, no `SuccessExitStatus=` and no `Restart=`, so Q-7's "no unit edit needed" is sound. HEAD really does exit 0 in both states Q-8 names: with `gained` and a failing check, `regen_ok` is `False`, the restart is already gated, `restarted` stays `False`, `failed` is empty, so it prints `Done` and falls off the end; with a failing restart, `restarted = True` is set unconditionally at `:2810` and the run claims a restart it did not get. Q-3's "the fix reaches OpenRC anyway" also holds in code, which stage 2 asserted without showing: `cmd_update_interval():2896` writes `#!/bin/sh\n/usr/local/bin/sc update-rules\n`, and a `sh` script's status is its last command's.

The one claim that did not survive is RS-2. Stage 2 files it as a residual for `07`: on a host with neither `SYSTEMD` nor `OPENRC`, `restart_service()` returns `True` and `sc update-rules` "still says restarted". It cannot. The call is guarded by `if regen_ok and is_running():` at `:2804`, and `is_running():2007` falls through to `return False` when neither constant is set — so `restart_service()` is never reached from that command on such a host, `restarted` stays `None`, and branch (d) prints "the service was not touched", which is true. I-4's justification for the `True` arm ("HEAD's behaviour on such a host is preserved") is trivially true rather than load-bearing, and its companion claim ("making it `False` would introduce a non-zero exit in a state nobody asked about") is false, because the value reaches no expression. The choice is harmless; letting a follow-up pool row describe an unreachable state is not, which is why C-7 stops it travelling as written. This is the T-17 pattern the dispatch asked me to look for, arriving in a residual rather than in a design row.

## The published-surface check, and why it is a condition rather than a rollback

T-18's precedent is that a design can be right in code and wrong in what the project has published. I looked for that shape and found it in the place `01` and `02` between them do not cover: `HELP_EN:2973` reads `status  Show service status, TUN interface, active node, egress IP`, and `HELP_ZH:3039` is its mirror. That is the same enumeration `README.md:245` carries — the one FR-10 *does* name — and `sc help` is read more often than the README. Neither FR-10's list of surfaces nor the change ledger's fourteen rows touches it.

I made it C-1 rather than a rollback to stage 1 because the edit is two literal lines inside `bin/sc`, a file AC-S3 already admits, requiring no translation key (the help blocks are printed directly, not through `t()`), and because FR-10's own intent — the docs describe shipped behaviour in the same commit — plainly reaches it. Spending a stage-1 round on two lines would be the successive-patching cost rule 85 exists to avoid, in the process rather than in the code. The wording gap in FR-10 goes upstream as a note so the next task's requirement author enumerates the in-binary help alongside the READMEs.

I checked the rest of the published surface and found nothing else falsified: `README*.md:291` and `:295` make no exit-status claim about `sc update-rules`, the `sc doctor` exit table at `:272-278` is untouched, and the doctor rule-set row at `:261` ("plus the byte count from that same read") stays accurate because K-17 adds no doctor row.

One vocabulary item I deliberately did **not** condition: `cmd_update_rules():2803` prints `Rule-sets restored:` for the `gained` set while `CONTEXT.md` fixes **gained** as the term and lists "restored" under `_Avoid_`. The design edits that statement's guard, so a gate could argue the correction is free. It is not free — it churns a translation key pair and breaks FR-9's freeze on the existing output contract for a word this task did not introduce. It goes to `07` as a pool row (F-8), which is where a pre-existing vocabulary drift belongs.

## Why the acceptance criteria needed C-3 and C-4 despite R-22 being honoured

`01` clearly took R-22 seriously: AC-B1, AC-B5 and AC-B6 each carry a HEAD control that disagrees, AC-B7 is labelled a freeze in both documents, and AC-B9 is protected against substitution by an artifact read in three separate places. I verified the three discriminating controls against HEAD's code rather than accepting them, and all three hold. That is a materially better AC set than T-15's.

Two gaps remain, both of the shape `.harness/insight-index.md:26` records. AC-B4 is a freeze — HEAD's emitted config is timestamp-independent for the trivial reason that HEAD never reads a timestamp — and it is not labelled as one, so a stage-6 report could list it beside AC-B1 as though both observed the change. It is a genuinely valuable guard after the widening lands; it is simply not evidence. AC-B8 is worse in kind: it spans V-8 and V-9 (disagreeing) and V-10 and V-11 (agreeing) under one criterion, which is precisely the bundling insight 26 says can only produce an agreeing control no matter how good the rig. C-3 splits the reporting rather than the criteria, because the criteria themselves are fine — it is the report that has to carry the class.

C-4 is a different failure: not a bundled class but a stated mechanism that is not the operative one. V-3 realises BC-6 with "nothing listening on the fixture's `clash_api_port`", and with `SYSTEMD = OPENRC = False` the port is never consulted — `is_running()` returns `False` at `:2007` without asking anything, and the section prints because K-6 put it above the gate. The green would be real and its reason false, which is how R-22(b) happened. The honest observation is the pair: the section prints in both `is_running()` states. I stated the property and left the mechanism to stage 6 deliberately, because the cheap route (stubbing `is_running`, `clash_api` and `_egress_ip` in-process, no subprocess, no network) is a design choice that belongs to whoever writes the fixture, not to me.

Separately, I checked the two vacuity traps this project keeps hitting and found the plan immune to both, though for a reason it does not state. `LANG` and `CLASH_PORT` are reassigned in `main()` (`:3127-3131`), and the plan drives no step through `main()` — but the plan then says the fixture's `settings.json` carries `clash_api_port`, which does nothing when `main()` is not driven, because `_resolve_clash_port()` never runs and `cmd_status():2240` prints the module global. That global is defined at `:326` as `CLASH_PORT = CLASH_PORT_BASE`, so there is no `NameError` and no harm — but the mitigation named in the plan is inert, and inert mitigations are how a later task convinces itself it is protected. It is folded into C-4's mechanism sentence rather than given its own condition. The `generate_config()` same-path trap (insight 12) is correctly handled: V-6 says "at the **same** fixture path" explicitly, and `RULES_DIR` really is emitted verbatim into `route.rule_set[].path` at `:1785`.

## The safety call, and why it is a condition and not `BLOCKED: NEEDS-HUMAN`

AC-B6 needs `SYSTEMD = True`, and `is_running()` under that flag shells `systemctl is-active --quiet sing-box` against the live host; a `True` from it would drive `restart_service()` into `systemctl restart sing-box` on the owner's machine. K-19 sees this and says so. What K-19 does not close is the mechanism.

`docs/dev-map.md`'s recipe shims **`os`** into `sys.modules` before exec'ing `bin/sc`, which is why its own warning says monkeypatching `sc.os.replace` patches the shim only. `subprocess` gets no such treatment: `bin/sc`'s `import subprocess` binds the real module object into `sc.__dict__`, so `sc.subprocess.run = stub` mutates the module for the whole interpreter — the harness included — and nothing in K-19 requires the stub to be *closed*. A stub written as "match these argv, else delegate" is the natural first draft and is exactly the shape that reaches a real `systemctl restart`. Two fixes are needed and both are cheap: shadow the name (`sc.subprocess = <stub module>`, leaving the real module untouched), and make the stub total — canned result per enumerated argv, raise on anything else.

I considered `BLOCKED: NEEDS-HUMAN` here, since the dispatch names this as the one legitimate place for it, and rejected it. The red line is not crossed by the design; it is left one careless fixture away, and the distance is closable by a constraint the developer and QA can be held to and stage 5 can read off the fixture source. That is a condition, not an escalation. C-5 carries it, and it travels to `07_DELIVERY.md` so the residual is visible after the pipeline forgets why it was written — the T-17 arrangement.

The rest of the safety floor I checked and found sound. `_init_files()` is never driven and is reachable only from `main()` (`:3129`); the auto-elevate at `:116-117` is neutralised by the recipe's `geteuid` shim rather than by editing the source; `/usr/local/bin/sc` is never invoked; and AC-S3's third clause makes the whole floor an observation (`verify_all` before and after, plus a command-log review) rather than a promise.
